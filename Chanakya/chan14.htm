Chanakya Niti

Chapter 14

                               Adhyāya 14

14-01

> पृथिव्यां त्रीणि रत्नानि जलमन्नं सुभाषितम् ।
> मूढैः पाषाणखण्डेषु रत्नसंज्ञा विधीयते ॥ १४-०१ ॥

On this earth, there are three true gems: water, food, and well-spoken speech.
But fools have attributed the designation of gems to mere fragments of stone.


14-02

> आत्मापराधवृक्षस्य फलान्येतानि देहिनाम् ।
> दारिद्र्यदुःखरोगाणि बन्धनव्यसनानि च ॥ १४-०२ ॥

The fruits of the tree of one's own transgressions are these for embodied
beings: poverty, sorrow, disease, bondage, and calamities.


14-03

> पुनर्वित्तं पुनर्मित्रं पुनर्भार्या पुनर्मही ।
> एतत्सर्वं पुनर्लभ्यं न शरीरं पुनः पुनः ॥ १४-०३ ॥

Wealth returns again, friends return again, a wife returns again, and even the
earth returns again; but the body, once gone, does not return again.


14-04

> बहूनां चैव सत्त्वानां समवायो रिपुञ्जयः ।
> वर्षाधाराधरो मेघस्तृणैरपि निवार्यते ॥ १४-०४ ॥

Even a large assembly of many creatures can conquer an enemy, just like a
dense mass of clouds laden with rain is blocked even by blades of grass.


14-05

> जले तैलं खले गुह्यं पात्रे दानं मनागपि ।
> प्राज्ञे शास्त्रं स्वयं याति विस्तारं वस्तुशक्तितः ॥ १४-०५ ॥

Oil in water, a secret with a wicked man, a gift, however small, to a worthy
recipient, and knowledge imparted to a wise man — these expand of their own
accord, by the very power of the object itself.


14-06

> धर्माख्याने श्मशाने च रोगिणां या मतिर्भवेत् ।
> सा सर्वदैव तिष्ठेच्चेत्को न मुच्येत बन्धनात् ॥ १४-०६ ॥

If the mind that arises in a person while hearing a religious discourse, in a
cremation ground, or when afflicted with disease were to remain at all times,
who would not be freed from bondage?


14-07

> उत्पन्नपश्चात्तापस्य बुद्धिर्भवति यादृशी ।
> तादृशी यदि पूर्वं स्यात्कस्य न स्यान्महोदयः ॥ १४-०७ ॥

If the wisdom that arises as a result of subsequent remorse were to have
existed beforehand, for whom would there not be great prosperity?


14-08

> दाने तपसि शौर्ये वा विज्ञाने विनये नये ।
> विस्मयो नहि कर्तव्यो बहुरत्ना वसुन्धरा ॥ १४-०८ ॥

One should never feel amazement in this world at the charity, austerity,
heroism, knowledge, humility, or statesmanship of another; the earth is rich
with many gems.


14-09

> दूरस्थोऽपि न दूरस्थो यो यस्य मनसि स्थितः ।
> यो यस्य हृदये नास्ति समीपस्थोऽपि दूरतः ॥ १४-०९ ॥

He who dwells in the mind of another is not far, even if physically distant;
but he who does not reside in another's heart is far away, even if he stands
close by.


14-10

> यस्माच्च प्रियमिच्छेत्तु तस्य ब्रूयात्सदा प्रियम् ।
> व्याधो मृगवधं कर्तुं गीतं गायति सुस्वरम् ॥ १४-१० ॥

One should always speak words that are pleasant to the person from whom one
desires something pleasant. A hunter, in order to kill a deer, sings a song
with a melodious voice.


14-11

> अत्यासन्ना विनाशाय दूरस्था न फलप्रदा ।
> सेव्यतां मध्यभावेन राजा वह्निर्गुरुः स्त्रियः ॥ १४-११ ॥

One who is excessively close leads to ruin; one who is at too great a distance
yields no fruit. Therefore, a king, fire, a preceptor, and women should be
served from a middle position.


14-12

> अग्निरापः स्त्रियो मूर्खाः सर्पा राजकुलानि च ।
> नित्यं यत्नेन सेव्यानि सद्यः प्राणहराणि षट् ॥ १४-१२ ॥

Fire, water, women, fools, serpents, and royal families — these six must
constantly be served with utmost care, for they are swift to take one's life.


14-13

> स जीवति गुणा यस्य यस्य धर्मः स जीवति ।
> गुणधर्मविहीनस्य जीवितं निष्प्रयोजनम् ॥ १४-१३ ॥

He lives who has virtues; he lives who has righteousness. The life of one
devoid of virtue and righteousness is utterly fruitless.


14-14

> यदीच्छसि वशीकर्तुं जगदेकेन कर्मणा ।
> पुरा पञ्चदशास्येभ्यो गां चरन्तीं निवारय ॥ १४-१४ ॥

If you wish to subdue the entire world with a single act, then hold back the
cow that is grazing the ripening grain from the mouths of the fifteen senses.


14-15

> प्रस्तावसदृशं वाक्यं प्रभावसदृशं प्रियम् ।
> आत्मशक्तिसमं कोपं यो जानाति स पण्डितः ॥ १४-१५ ॥

He is a wise man who knows words that suit the occasion, affection that
matches one's influence, and anger that is commensurate with one's own
strength.


14-16

> एक एव पदार्थस्तु त्रिधा भवति वीक्षितः ।
> कुणपं कामिनी मांसं योगिभिः कामिभिः श्वभिः ॥ १४-१६ ॥

One and the same material object is perceived in three different ways: as a
corpse by a Yogin, as a beloved woman by a lustful man, and as a piece of meat
by dogs.


14-17

> सुसिद्धमौषधं धर्मं गृहच्छिद्रं च मैथुनम् ।
> कुभुक्तं कुश्रुतं चैव मतिमान्न प्रकाशयेत् ॥ १४-१७ ॥

A well-prepared medicine, a righteous deed, domestic secrets, sexual
intercourse, badly cooked food, and badly heard learning — a wise man should
not reveal these.


14-18

> तावन्मौनेन नीयन्ते कोकिलैश्चैव वासराः ।
> यावत्सर्वजनानन्ददायिनी वाक्प्रवर्तते ॥ १४-१८ ॥

One should spend one's days in silence, like the cuckoo, only until such time
as one's speech can issue forth, giving delight to all people.


14-19

> धर्मं धनं च धान्यं च गुरोर्वचनमौषधम् ।
> सुगृहीतं च कर्तव्यमन्यथा तु न जीवति ॥ १४-१९ ॥

Righteousness, wealth, grain, the words of the preceptor, and medicine — one
should take these only after proper consideration; otherwise, one simply
cannot live.


14-20

> त्यज दुर्जनसंसर्गं भज साधुसमागमम् ।
> कुरु पुण्यमहोरात्रं स्मर नित्यमनित्यतः ॥ १४-२० ॥

Shun the company of the wicked; cherish the association of the virtuous. Do
meritorious deeds day and night, and constantly recall the impermanence of all
things.
